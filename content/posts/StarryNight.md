" They say that, whenever you see the stars fall, if you wish upon them to live, you'll get to see next morning's sun. "
{{</lead>}}

Even long after the war the stories of a monster so precise, so reckless, that hunted those foolish enough to venture into the forest at night stayed. Though now it was nothing but a myth that few remembered where it originated from, some swore it still held true.

And Ant hated it. Hated what it meant because he would never forget it, because it forever tainted his very being. But it was useful. People expected to meet a reckless monster, and he didn't want to be found, so keeping them at bay through fear was effective. However, he rarely brought himself to display any aggressiveness, his strong build and sharp features where more than enough to scare anyone away. Well, almost anyone.

### Extra notes

I was thinking of Ant at work and I kept picturing him, standing on his own in the middle of the night, so his people can sleep peacefully. Pushing the enemy lines back and keeping them at bay while the sun is down. His blindness becoming a tactical advantage as he doesn't rely on light to see. While his fire magic, polished to be more of an spectacle than an attack, falls in form of stars that temporarily blind those who dare get close enough, and in the worst cases, even burn them. Not fully of course, but enough to scare and sometimes scar.
