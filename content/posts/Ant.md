In short, it's a Monster Tenna story based on Undertale but set in a more modernized world, and it reinterprets the war and some aspects of it. It's also loosely based on the Legend of Hei (especially the theme of the battle between spirits and humans).
The years don't match to irl events but its based on that for like technological reference points and such, like when Ant starts his career is around 1950-ish. Also he's a boss monster and that aging logic applies.
## Timeline

Ant's story is divided into three parts, with the war between humans and monsters serving as the common thread.

### Before the War
Before the war, Ant worked in entertainment. He was born into a prominent family of insectoid monsters, a family that had always been very sociable. But Ant was the first to delve deeper into the human world, fascinated by their art, especially their music, and by the fact that they used sounds rather than magic to enhance their performances, since Ant was born blind, just like his mother.

His affable and friendly demeanour, paired with his natural charisma, quickly earned him a good reputation in the industry, and he eventually went on to host his own children’s shows aimed at educating both humans and monsters. He believed this would help bridge the social divide between the two over time. During this time, he met many people and even travelled around the world; it was a very hectic period where he was always on edge, but he enjoyed the intensity of it all and how much he could learn about human culture.

However, all this fame threatened to vanish overnight. At first, not quite sure why, he frantically reached out to everyone he knew, searching for an explanation. No one could give him an answer until a mysterious but charming man, short in stature but with a great personality, named Spencer explained which trends he should follow to maintain his popularity. This was a huge help, as thanks to him the studio managed to stay afloat, but the audience still wasn’t what it used to be. Human-monster relations, due to various political tensions, were on a steady decline.

Ant, aware of this, pleaded one last time to Spencer for information. The human, terrified and worried for Ant due to the increasingly real threat of war, tried to uncover the details of how the war would begun. In that very last attempt to obtain intelligence, and despite his informant and benefactor’s warning, he was caught red-handed and deemed a spy against humanity.

### During the War

The monster, now with his closest companion gone and the studio in ruins, had to retreat to live in a village, taken in by old friends, both humans and monsters. They gave him a home, and in return, he did what he did best: entertaining and educating the children, shielding them from the danger lurking in more distant cities and the tension this caused among the adults.

The war eventually reached even that remote town, and Ant, despite being a boss monster of considerable power, was unable to strike back at the humans, instead focusing on defending the children, the monsters, and the other humans who stayed behind to fight alongside him. However, that wasn’t enough, and he was eventually overwhelmed by firearms. He was rescued and saved by the very children he was protecting, who, with the support of their parents, hid him in a shed and helped stabilize him with weak healing magic.

Even after such a fierce battle, the monsters were forced to abandon the surface, tearing apart many relationships,  including entire families from that village. But it was a gradual process. Although a large number retreated that very day, just as many were driven out later or even managed to remain on the surface. Some went into hiding or sought refuge among sympathetic humans, but their nature made this a difficult task. So much so that many monsters on the surface eventually turned themselves in of their own accord, unable to live in the shadows and the constant threat to be hunted.

### After the War
However, Ant was unable to leave the village. It lay completely destroyed and abandoned, no one had thought to go back to even scavenge the remains, but he, initially with the help of some of the children who had rescued him, stayed behind.

For the first few months, he was physically unable to move, or at least not without intense pain. His recovery saved him, but it wasn’t enough to restore him to his former self, and now that human magic had been sealed away, they couldn’t help him directly. In the months that followed, he gained some independence, but deeply affected by the war, he still needed help to function.

But the children grew up and had responsibilities of their own. Ant knew this and didn’t want to burden them with his situation, and when they eventually left him alone, he didn't beg any of them to stay, as much as he wanted to. Still unable to let go of what little remained of his former life, he stayed behind to protect what was left of the village, thanks in part to what he had learned while traveling the world, and in part by raiding anyone who came too close.
